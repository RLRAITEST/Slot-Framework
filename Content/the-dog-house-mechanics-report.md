# The Dog House (Pragmatic Play, 2019) — Full Mechanics & Math Report

A teardown of the game's structure, win logic, and math model, written from a game-design/implementation perspective.

---

## 1. Headline specification

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Studio / release | Pragmatic Play, 2019 |
| Grid | 5 reels × 3 rows |
| Win evaluation | 20 fixed paylines, left-to-right from reel 1 |
| Default RTP | 96.51% (operator-selectable variants exist, e.g. 95.51%) |
| Volatility | High (rated 5/5 by the studio) |
| Max win | 6,750× total bet |
| Bet range | ~0.20 – 100 (currency units), 20 lines always active |
| Feature count | One — sticky multiplier wild free spins |

The design thesis is worth stating up front: **this game deliberately has only one mechanic.** No tumbles, no cluster pays, no cascading multiplier meter, no picker bonus. Everything — the volatility profile, the max win, the emotional arc — is generated by a single rule (sticky multiplier wilds constrained to three reels). That constraint is why it still gets cloned seven years later.

---

## 2. Symbol set

13 distinct symbols:

**Premiums (4–5 dog symbols)** — Doberman is the top payer at 37.5× total bet for 5-of-a-kind. Poodle and Pug sit around 15×, Beagle around 10×. (Exact values vary slightly between sources; treat these as indicative and verify against the in-game paytable if you need them exact.)

**Mid-tier** — a dog collar/lead and a bone/treat symbol, roughly in the 5–7× band for 5OAK.

**Low-tier royals** — 10, J, Q, K, A, paying roughly 1.25–2.5× for 5OAK.

**Wild — the kennel/dog house.** Substitutes for everything except the scatter. **Appears only on reels 2, 3 and 4.** Never on the outer reels.

**Scatter — the jewelled paw print.** **Appears only on reels 1, 3 and 5.** Pays as a scatter in its own right: three of them award 5× total bet *and* trigger the feature.

Note the reel-restriction structure. The wild reel set (2,3,4) and the scatter reel set (1,3,5) intersect only at reel 3. This is not decoration — it's the load-bearing constraint of the whole math model, and I unpack why in §7.

---

## 3. Payline evaluation

Standard left-aligned line pays: a combination must start on reel 1 and occupy consecutive reels. 20 fixed lines, non-configurable, so `total bet = line bet × 20` and all paytable values are conventionally quoted as multiples of total bet.

Only the highest win per line is paid. Multiple lines pay independently and sum.

---

## 4. The wild multiplier system (base game)

Every wild that lands carries a random multiplier value, revealed on landing:

- Multiplier values are **2× or 3×** (some marketing copy says "1×, 2× or 3×", which effectively means a plain no-multiplier wild is also possible on some builds — check the specific build's rules).
- All wilds landing **on the same reel share the same multiplier value** on that spin.
- The multiplier applies to any line win the wild participates in.

### The critical rule: multipliers ADD, they don't multiply

This is the single most commonly mis-stated fact about the game, including on several review sites. When more than one wild contributes to the same winning line, their multiplier values are **summed**:

```
3 wilds on one line, each 3× →  3 + 3 + 3 = 9×   (NOT 27×)
```

Hence the frequently quoted "maximum win multiplier of ×9". With only three wild-eligible reels and a max value of 3×, the ceiling on any single line's multiplier is `3 × 3 = 9`.

This matters enormously for the math model. Additive stacking is *linear* in wild count; multiplicative stacking is exponential. Pragmatic chose additive precisely to keep the tail bounded and the max win at a marketable-but-modelable 6,750× rather than something in the tens of thousands.

### Base-game win formula

For a line `L` with base paytable value `P(L)` (in units of total bet), and wilds `w ∈ W(L)` participating in that line:

```
win(L) = P(L) × max(1, Σ_{w ∈ W(L)} m(w))
total_win = Σ_L win(L)
```

Where `m(w)` is the wild's multiplier value. The `max(1, …)` guard handles lines with no wild.

## 5. Free spins trigger

Three paw scatters landing simultaneously on reels 1, 3 and 5 trigger the feature. Because the scatter is reel-restricted to exactly three reels and exactly three are required, **the trigger is effectively a binary "all three or nothing" event** — you cannot land 4 or 5 scatters, so there are no tiered trigger sizes.

Design consequence: the near-miss surface is tiny and legible. The player watches reel 1, then reel 3, then reel 5. It's a clean three-beat anticipation sequence, which is why the game feels tense despite having almost no features. Compare to a scatter-anywhere game where anticipation is muddy.

The trigger also pays 5× total bet as a scatter win, layered on top of whatever the line pays produced.

## 6. Free spins count — the 3×3 grid

Before the feature starts, the game cuts to a shed scene where a Doberman spins a nine-cell mechanical grid. **Each of the 9 cells independently reveals 1, 2 or 3 free spins.** The nine values are summed.

```
free_spins = Σ_{i=1..9} c_i,   c_i ∈ {1, 2, 3}
Range: 9 … 27
```

If the cells were uniform and independent, the distribution would be a sum of 9 i.i.d. discrete uniforms — mean 18, roughly bell-shaped, with 9 and 27 each at probability 3⁻⁹ ≈ 1 in 19,683. In practice the underlying weights are almost certainly non-uniform and skewed toward the low end, since the free-spin count is one of the main RTP tuning knobs in the feature. Do not assume uniform if you're modelling this.

**The feature cannot be retriggered.** Scatters do not land during free spins on the original title. (Some later entries in the series changed this; the 2019 original does not retrigger.)

This is a deliberate and slightly unusual choice: a bounded, known-length feature. The player knows on spin 1 exactly how many spins they have, which converts the bonus from an open-ended "hope it keeps going" into a closed-form "how much can I fill the grid in N spins" problem. That's a materially different emotional shape, and it's cleaner to model.

## 7. Sticky wilds — the core loop

During free spins, the rule change is exactly one line long:

> **Every wild that lands on reels 2, 3 or 4 locks in place for the remainder of the feature, retaining its revealed multiplier.**

Everything interesting follows from this.

### The accumulation dynamic

Wild-eligible positions total **9** (3 reels × 3 rows). Each free spin, some subset of the currently-empty wild positions gets filled. Since wilds never leave, the grid state is monotonically increasing — the board can only get better.

Let `S_t` = set of occupied sticky positions after spin `t`. Then `S_0 ⊆ S_1 ⊆ … ⊆ S_N`, and the win on spin `t` is a non-decreasing function of `|S_t|` in expectation.

This produces the game's signature payout curve: **the bonus is back-loaded.** Early spins pay little; the last third of the feature is where the value lands. A 27-spin draw is worth disproportionately more than 3× a 9-spin draw, because the extra spins are spent on a fuller board. Any simulation that treats free spins as independent identically-distributed events will badly underestimate the tail.

### Why the reel restriction is load-bearing

Wilds only landing on reels 2, 3, 4 means:

1. **Reels 1 and 5 must still be won honestly.** A fully wild-filled middle cannot pay anything by itself — you always need a real symbol on reel 1 to start the line. This is what keeps the feature from degenerating.
2. **The multiplier ceiling is hard-capped at 9×** (3 reels × 3× max, additive). No amount of luck breaks it.
3. **Full-grid saturation is achievable.** With 9 positions and up to 27 spins, filling the whole middle is uncommon but reachable — which is exactly where the max win lives.

### Free-spins win formula

Identical to the base formula, with the wild set being persistent:

```
win_t(L) = P(L) × max(1, Σ_{w ∈ (S_t ∩ L)} m(w))
```

Where `S_t` includes all previously stuck wilds plus any landing this spin.

## 8. Deriving the 6,750× max win

The headline figure isn't arbitrary — it falls straight out of the rules, and reproducing the derivation is a good sanity check on any model:

```
Top symbol 5OAK (Doberman)          =  37.5×
Max line multiplier (3 wilds × 3×)  =   9×   (additive)
Paylines all paying simultaneously  =  20
                                       ─────────────
37.5 × 9 × 20                       = 6,750×
```

The required board state: **all 9 middle positions occupied by sticky wilds, every one carrying a 3× multiplier, with reels 1 and 5 fully stacked with the Doberman.** Every one of the 20 paylines then reads as a 5-of-a-kind top symbol at 9×.

This exact-fit derivation confirms two things independently: multipliers are definitely additive, and the max win is a single-spin event on a saturated board rather than a cumulative feature total.

Realistically this is a once-in-tens-of-millions-of-spins outcome. The vast majority of bonus rounds land somewhere in the 5×–100× band.

## 9. RTP and volatility profile

**RTP:** 96.51% default. Pragmatic ships multiple certified builds (commonly a ~95.5% variant, sometimes lower) and operators choose which to deploy. The rules and math structure are identical across variants — only the reelstrip weightings change. Worth knowing if you're benchmarking against public data: the RTP you observe in a demo may not be the RTP of the build you're comparing against.

**Volatility:** rated high / 5-of-5. The structural reasons:

- Base game does most of its work with low-value line hits and the occasional 2×/3× wild boost — insufficient to sustain a bankroll.
- The trigger is restrictive (three specific reels, all-or-nothing), so bonus frequency is low.
- Feature value is concentrated in the back half of the round and in high-multiplier saturation, which is itself rare.

The result is a payout distribution with a large mass near zero, a modest bump around break-even, and a long thin tail — textbook bonus-hunter shape. The game's own marketing is candid about this: base-game play is largely a queue for the feature.

**RTP split:** not officially published for the original, but given the structure, expect the large majority of returned value to sit in the free spins round, with the base game contributing a minority share. Modelling it as roughly a quarter base / three quarters feature is a reasonable starting hypothesis to calibrate against.

## 10. Implementation model

If you were building this, the state machine is small:

```
STATES: BASE → (trigger) → WHEEL → FREESPINS → BASE

BASE:
  spin reelstrips[base]          # wild weights only on reels 2,3,4
                                 # scatter weights only on reels 1,3,5
  for each wild-bearing reel r:
      m[r] ← sample({2,3}, weights)     # one value per reel, shared
  evaluate 20 lines with additive multiplier rule
  if scatters on reels 1,3,5:
      pay 5× total bet
      → WHEEL

WHEEL:
  N ← Σ_{i=1..9} sample({1,2,3}, weights)   # NOT uniform — tuning knob
  sticky ← {}                                # 9-slot grid, positions on reels 2-4
  → FREESPINS

FREESPINS:
  repeat N times:
      spin reelstrips[fs]                    # no scatters on strips
      for each new wild landing on reels 2,3,4:
          m ← sample({2,3}, weights)
          sticky.add(position, m)
      render board = spun symbols, overwritten by sticky at sticky positions
      evaluate 20 lines with additive multiplier over sticky ∪ new
  → BASE
```

Points that are easy to get wrong:

- **Per-reel multiplier, not per-wild.** In the base game all wilds on the same reel share one value. During free spins, each sticky wild retains the value it was revealed with, so a mature board legitimately has mixed values on the same reel.
- **Sticky wilds must be composited over the spun result**, not injected into the reelstrip. The strip still spins underneath; the sticky positions simply override.
- **Wild-position occupancy is not independent across spins** once the round is running. This is the thing that breaks naïve RTP estimation.
- **Free-spin count distribution is a primary RTP lever.** Along with wild landing frequency on the FS strips and the 2×/3× split, it's one of only three real knobs. That's a very small tuning surface — which is a feature, not a bug, when you're trying to hit a target RTP without the model becoming unanalysable.
- **Simulate the whole round as one unit.** Because value accumulates within a round, per-spin RTP is meaningless here. Your simulation unit should be a complete bonus round from trigger to exit.

## 11. Sequels — what each one changed

Useful as a catalogue of "which knob did they turn":

| Title | Change to the core |
|---|---|
| **Megaways** (2020) | 117,649 ways, 2–7 symbols per reel; wilds extend to reel 5; two selectable FS modes (Sticky Wilds vs Raining Wilds); RTP ~96.55%, max ~27,300× |
| **Multihold** | Multiple simultaneous grids sharing the hold/sticky mechanic |
| **Royal Hunt** | Adds a scatter nudge/respin mechanic before trigger; retains 3×3 wheel and no-retrigger sticky wilds; max ~8,000× |
| **Dog or Alive** (2024) | Retrigger via wild count thresholds (3/6/9 wilds → +3 spins and *doubles all on-screen multipliers*); bonus buy at 120× |
| **Muttley Crew** | Theme reskin, pirate setting |
| **The Big Dog House** (2026) | "3 Pot" modifier structure; wilds in 1×1/1×3/3×3 sizes; adds a Ghost Wild on reel 5 that *removes* a random sticky wild; medium volatility, ~96.53% RTP, 15,000× max, ante bets and 100×/200×/500× buys |

The Big Dog House is the most instructive one: adding a mechanic that *removes* sticky wilds is the first time the series broke its own monotonicity guarantee, and it's what let them push the max win to 15,000× while pulling volatility down to medium.

## 12. Design takeaways

Things worth stealing:

1. **One mechanic, executed hard.** The entire game is "sticky multiplier wilds on three reels." Everything else is presentation. The mechanic is explainable in one sentence and the player understands it in one bonus round.

2. **Constrain the wild to a subset of reels.** This simultaneously caps the max multiplier, guarantees the outer reels still have to be earned, and creates a visually legible "fill the middle" goal. Three reels × three rows = 9 slots is a very readable progress bar.

3. **Additive multiplier stacking for a controllable tail.** Multiplicative stacking sounds better in marketing but makes the tail nearly impossible to bound. Additive gives a clean, derivable max win.

4. **Bounded feature length with a revealed count.** The 3×3 wheel converts an unknown-duration bonus into a known one, and gives you a whole extra anticipation beat before the feature even starts — a ceremony that costs almost nothing to build and buys real emotional value.

5. **A max win that falls out of the rules.** 37.5 × 9 × 20 = 6,750. The number isn't tuned into existence; it's the arithmetic ceiling of the ruleset. That's a good sign the math model is coherent.
